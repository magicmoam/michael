Previously, On El Vrada

Well... It Happened. The Border Is OPEN.

April 10th came. April 10th went. The Gibraltar-Spain border opened, exactly as threatened. No queue. No passport stamp. No fence. You just... walk into Spain. Like it's allowed. Like it's NORMAL.

El Vrada has had three months to process this. He has not processed this.

El Vrada:

"This is it, mate. This is the selling of our country. SOLD. Gift-wrapped. What has Picardo DONE?! I want to see the receipt. I want a REFUND."

El Vrada:

"That border wasn't a queue, it was a FILTER. Now anything can just wander in. And worse — anything can wander OUT."

The Announcement

"Dad, I'm Going To The Feria With The Boys"

Enter Jyzon. Sixteen. El Vrada's pride, joy, and primary source of blood pressure. It's Feria week in La Línea — lights, casetas, churros, fairground rides assembled by men who looked confident enough — and Jyzon's whole squad is going.

Jyzon:

"Dad, it's literally a ten minute walk now. There's not even a border. Everyone's going. It's FINE."

And there it is. Every parent's nightmare. For El Vrada's entire life, the border meant one beautiful thing: your kids couldn't get far. The Rock was a playpen with a frontier for a fence. Jyzon at a loose end? He's at the Piazza. He's at Casemates. He is, at absolute worst, at Eastern Beach.

Not any more. Now "out with my friends" has a radius that includes the entire Iberian Peninsula.

El Vrada:

"That border was the best babysitter in EUROPE. Free. Reliable. Open 24 hours. And Picardo gave it away. Where's the sad face on the news for the PARENTS, eh? WHERE?"

El Vrada, in his good shirt, ironed by his mum, walking across the airfield towards the lights of La Línea. Not going. Absolutely not going. Currently going.

"I'm not GOING going. I'm chaperoning. It's a security operation. There's a DIFFERENCE, and the difference is that I don't want to be here."

2:00 AM

The Fence That Once Was

The lights are going out, one caseta at a time. Jyzon walks ahead with Winston under his arm, texting someone called "M 🌹" (a separate nightmare, filed for later). And El Vrada stops.

Right there. Where the fence used to be. Fifteen Cruzcampos deep, he stands at the line only he can still see, and looks across it.

x---x---x---x---x---x---x---x---x---x | | | | | | | | | | x---x---x---x---x---x---x---x---x---x | | | | | | | | | | ( artist's impression — it's not there any more )

No queue to complain about. No guard to nod at. No stamp. Just a warm night breeze moving freely — freely — between two countries, like it doesn't even know what it's crossing.

*He puts one hand up, gently, against a chain-link fence that is not there. It hasn't been there since April.*

El Vrada:

"You were a terrible fence. Ugly. Slow. Made me miss two weddings and a christening. ...I miss you every day."

😢
*A single tear. This time, everybody can tell exactly what it's from.*

"One minute, son. Show some respect. ...People QUEUED here."
